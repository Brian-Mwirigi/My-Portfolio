I'm Brian. Second year of CS at Strathmore, Nairobi.

A while back I sat in my room at night and asked an AI a question I was too embarrassed to ask a real person. What is the actual difference between me and the CS students at Stanford and MIT who are my age and already raising millions?

I wanted it to say luck. Or rich parents. Or connections. Something that wasn't my fault.

It didn't say that.

Let me tell you where I was standing when I asked. I had a registered company, Corvux, with zero clients. The hospitals and SACCOs I'd been calling had stopped picking up. Not rejecting me, just not picking up, which is somehow worse. I had a credit scoring project that made semi finals of a competition and then we dropped it, because it was a demo and everyone on the team knew it was a demo. I'd done a hackathon remotely, built something in 48 hours, didn't win. A chrome extension with a few hundred users. Some CLI tools. Papers I self published that nobody read. A small web agency that pays my rent.

Written down like that it looks like a lot. That was the trap. It felt like a lot while I was doing it. Every single one of those things felt like progress on the day I started it.

The answer, when it came, fit in one sentence.

Those kids have one thing they went deep on. I have seven things I went shallow on.

Roughly the same hours. They put theirs into one hard problem until something came out the other side that nobody could argue with. A paper people cite. A system with real users. A number. I sprayed mine across a company, a demo, a hackathon, an agency, some tools, and became the guy who has done a lot of stuff and cannot point at one thing that survives a serious look.

And nobody made them focus, by the way. Their environment did it for them. A professor fails you when you fake understanding. A lab tells you when your work is not good enough. Out here nobody was checking my work, so I got to feel productive for two years without anyone telling me I was walking in circles.

I didn't take it well. I cried, honestly. A grown man crying at his desk because a chatbot told him the truth about his own portfolio. I'm putting that here because pretending it didn't happen would be the exact kind of lying this page is supposed to end.

But if you sit with that answer long enough, it flips. Talent was never the problem. Output was never the problem. The problem was scattering, and scattering is a decision, and a decision can be reversed.

This page is the reversal.

Every week I post what I actually did. The code, the numbers, what broke, what I still don't understand. When a week goes badly, the post says the week went badly. No motivation posts. No plans dressed up as progress. No dead demos rebranded as traction. I've done enough of that for one lifetime.

The itch to start something new is what got me here. It will come back, and it will feel like inspiration, because it always does. When it shows up, this page will be sitting here in public, asking me one question.

Did this week get its hours?

That's why I started.
